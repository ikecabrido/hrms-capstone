# Employee Engagement and Relations Module of a Human Resource Management System

## Chapter 1: Introduction

### 1.1 Background of the Study

Human Resource Management Systems (HRMS) are essential tools for managing employees, processes, and organizational operations in a structured and efficient manner. As organizations continue to expand, there is a greater need for digital systems that not only handle administrative tasks but also support employee engagement and workplace relations. Employee engagement has become a crucial factor in employee satisfaction, productivity, and organizational performance. It is closely related to how employees perceive their workplace, how they communicate with management, and how they respond to organizational policies and processes.

The Employee Engagement and Relations module is a vital component of a modern HRMS because it provides a platform for communication, recognition, feedback, and grievance management. Through this module, employees can receive announcements, participate in surveys, express concerns, and acknowledge the achievements of their colleagues. At the same time, administrators and HR personnel can monitor employee engagement indicators, manage communication, and address issues in a timely and organized way.

This study focuses on the development of the Employee Engagement and Relations module as part of a broader HRMS. The project aims to improve the communication framework and strengthen employee relations by creating a digital platform that supports engagement processes within the organization. By integrating communication, recognition, feedback, and grievance handling into one system, the project seeks to promote a more collaborative and productive workplace environment.

### 1.2 Statement of the Problem

Many organizations still rely on traditional and fragmented methods for managing employee communication, recognition, and concern reporting. These methods often lead to delayed responses, poor communication, limited employee participation, and weak monitoring of engagement-related issues. Employees may struggle to submit feedback, raise grievances, or participate in organizational activities if the process is not properly centralized and digitalized.

In addition, without a systematic engagement platform, HR personnel may find it difficult to monitor employee concerns, analyze engagement trends, and respond to issues in a timely manner. This results in gaps in communication and weak employee relations, which can negatively affect morale, trust, and productivity. The absence of a structured and integrated system highlights the need for a digital solution that supports employee engagement and strengthens the relationship between employees and management.

### 1.3 Objectives of the Study

The main objective of this study is to develop an Employee Engagement and Relations module that supports employee communication and workplace engagement within the HRMS.

Specifically, the study aims to:

1. design and implement a centralized platform for announcements and employee communication;
2. provide a mechanism for survey participation and employee feedback collection;
3. support recognition and appreciation through awards, rewards, and badges;
4. establish a structured grievance submission and monitoring process;
5. strengthen employee involvement and improve communication between employees and management; and
6. ensure the efficient storage, retrieval, and management of engagement-related data.

### 1.4 Scope and Delimitation

The scope of this study includes the design and implementation of the Employee Engagement and Relations module within the HRMS. The system covers key features such as communication, notifications, surveys, feedback, recognition, grievances, and social collaboration. This study focuses on managing employee engagement functions in a centralized platform that can be accessed by employees and administrators.

The system is limited to the engagement module and does not cover the full operational scope of the entire HRMS. Other areas such as payroll, recruitment, timekeeping, learning, compliance, and clinic management are not the primary focus of this project. The study therefore emphasizes employee engagement and the improvement of employee relations within the organization.

### 1.5 Significance of the Study

The study is significant because it addresses the need for a structured and systematic approach to employee engagement within the organization. A centralized platform for communication, feedback, recognition, and grievance management can greatly improve employee satisfaction and strengthen workplace relationships. It also helps HR personnel monitor engagement-related concerns and respond to them more effectively.

The project is beneficial to employees because it provides a clear and accessible avenue for expressing feedback and raising concerns. It is also beneficial to administrators because it improves communication, promotes transparency, and supports decision-making based on collected engagement data. By improving employee engagement, the organization can strengthen morale, trust, and productivity, which ultimately contributes to organizational success.

### 1.6 Definition of Terms

- Employee Engagement: the emotional and intellectual commitment of employees toward their work and organization.
- Employee Relations: the relationship between employees and management, including communication, trust, and conflict resolution.
- HRMS: Human Resource Management System, a digital platform used to manage human resource functions.
- Grievance: a formal complaint or concern raised by an employee regarding workplace issues.
- Recognition: the acknowledgment of employee performance, effort, and achievements.
- Feedback: information provided by employees regarding their experience, opinions, or suggestions.

## Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature

### 2.1 Employee Engagement in Organizations

Employee engagement has become a major focus in modern human resource management because it directly influences organizational effectiveness. Engaged employees are more committed, productive, and motivated to contribute to the success of the organization. According to organizational theory, engagement is not simply about job satisfaction; it involves emotional attachment, commitment, and active participation in the workplace. Employees who feel valued and supported are more likely to perform effectively and remain loyal to the organization.

The concept of employee engagement is closely tied to communication, motivation, and workplace culture. Organizations that create supportive environments and provide clear channels for feedback often experience higher levels of engagement. These conditions allow employees to feel heard, recognized, and connected to organizational goals. As a result, employee engagement contributes to improved morale, stronger collaboration, and better overall performance.

### 2.2 Employee Relations and Organizational Culture

Employee relations refer to the relationship between employees and management, including communication, trust, and workplace cooperation. A healthy employee relations environment promotes fairness, respect, and open communication, which are important for reducing workplace conflict and improving morale. Organizations that invest in strong employee relations often foster a more positive and productive culture.

When employees feel respected and informed, they are more likely to trust management and participate actively in organizational initiatives. This is especially important in modern organizations where employee expectations include transparency, accountability, and timely communication. Effective employee relations are therefore essential for creating an environment that supports both organizational goals and employee well-being.

### 2.3 Human Resource Management Systems and Digital Transformation

The increasing use of digital technologies has transformed the way organizations manage human resources. Human Resource Management Systems provide centralized platforms for handling employee records, communication, attendance, performance, and organizational processes. These systems improve efficiency by automating repetitive tasks and allowing administrators to monitor employee information more effectively.

Within HRMS, engagement-related features are important because they allow organizations to manage communication and employee interaction in a structured manner. Features such as announcements, surveys, feedback collection, recognition, and grievance management support employee involvement and contribute to a healthier workplace culture. Digital systems help organizations respond faster to employee concerns and provide more transparent communication processes.

### 2.4 Communication and Employee Participation

Communication is one of the most important drivers of employee engagement. When employees are informed about company updates, goals, and policies, they are more likely to feel connected to the organization. Effective communication reduces uncertainty and helps align employee actions with organizational objectives. Communication also creates a sense of belonging, which is essential for long-term engagement.

Employee participation is often improved when employees have access to communication channels such as announcements, newsletters, and feedback systems. These tools allow employees to express their opinions and become more involved in organizational activities. In this way, communication acts as a bridge between employees and management and strengthens employee trust and collaboration.

### 2.5 Recognition, Rewards, and Motivation

Recognition is a critical component of employee engagement and motivation. Employees who feel appreciated for their work are more likely to remain committed and perform well. Recognition programs, rewards, and badges provide visible evidence that employee contributions are valued. These mechanisms can increase morale and encourage stronger performance across the organization.

Digital recognition systems are particularly useful because they can store records, track achievements, and make appreciation more visible within the workplace. By recognizing employee effort and accomplishments, organizations can create a positive culture that motivates employees to continue performing well and contributing to the organization’s goals.

### 2.6 Grievance Management and Employee Trust

Grievance management is an essential function of employee relations. Employees may face concerns related to policies, workload, communication, leadership, or working conditions. When these issues are not addressed properly, they can affect morale and productivity. A structured grievance system allows employees to raise concerns in a formal and transparent manner, which promotes fairness and improves trust in the organization.

A well-designed grievance process enables management to investigate concerns, document actions, and provide timely resolutions. This not only helps resolve issues but also signals to employees that their opinions are valued and that their concerns are taken seriously. As a result, grievance management contributes to a healthier and more respectful workplace environment.

### 2.7 Summary of Related Literature

The literature reviewed supports the importance of employee engagement, communication, recognition, and grievance resolution in improving employee relations. Digital systems such as HRMS and engagement platforms provide organizations with practical tools for managing these functions more effectively. By integrating engagement features into a single system, organizations can strengthen employee involvement, improve communication, and foster a more collaborative work environment.

This review supports the development of the proposed system, which aims to centralize engagement-related functions and improve employee experience within the HRMS.

## Chapter 3: Methodology

### 3.1 Research Design

This study follows a systems development approach, which focuses on the design, implementation, and evaluation of a functional information system. The project involves identifying user requirements, designing the system architecture, developing the modules, and testing the resulting application. The methodology is appropriate because the study aims to produce a working digital platform that supports employee engagement and relations.

### 3.2 System Development Process

The development process begins with analysis of the existing HRMS structure and user needs. Requirements are gathered based on the features needed for communication, feedback, recognition, and grievance management. Following the analysis phase, the system is designed using a modular structure that separates the user interface, business logic, and database functions.

The implementation phase then follows, where the pages, controllers, database connectivity, and API modules are developed. The project uses PHP as the server-side language and MySQL as the database management system. These technologies are suitable for building a secure and organized web-based HR system.

### 3.3 Data Collection and Requirements

The system requirements are derived from the major functions expected in an engagement system. These include the ability to post announcements, manage notifications, conduct surveys, collect feedback, present recognition records, and track grievances. The requirements also include access control for employees and administrators so that the system remains secure and relevant to the user’s role.

### 3.4 System Testing and Evaluation

System testing is conducted to verify that the implemented functions work properly and that the system meets its intended purpose. Testing includes validation of login, session handling, access control, survey interaction, feedback submission, recognition display, and grievance processing. The evaluation process checks whether the system meets the expected functional requirements and whether it supports employee engagement effectively.

### 3.5 Summary

The methodology used in this study provides a structured approach to system development. It ensures that the project is designed to address real user needs and organizational problems related to employee engagement and relations. The final system is expected to improve communication, participation, and workplace transparency through an integrated digital platform.

## Chapter 4: System Design

### 4.1 Overview of the System

The Employee Engagement and Relations module is designed to support employee interaction, communication, and organizational engagement within the HRMS. The system architecture follows a modular design in which different functions are grouped according to their purpose. This modular approach enables the system to be organized, manageable, and scalable for future expansion.

### 4.2 System Architecture

The architecture of the system is composed of several layers: the presentation layer, the application logic layer, and the data layer. The presentation layer includes the web pages used by employees and administrators. The application logic layer is responsible for handling requests, processing user actions, and performing business rules. The data layer stores records and supports retrieval and updates for system functions.

This layered design separates the interface from the core processing and database operations. It improves maintainability, allows easier debugging, and supports future enhancement of the HRMS. The engagement module is integrated into the broader system and is accessed through authentication, session management, page routing, and API-based data handling.

### 4.3 Functional Modules

The system is organized into functional modules that correspond to the main activities of employee engagement. These modules include communication, surveys, recognition, feedback, grievance management, and social collaboration. Each module is designed to perform a specific task while remaining connected to the overall HRMS.

The communication module enables HR personnel to post announcements and send notifications to employees. The survey module supports employee participation in surveys and allows the collection of feedback data. The recognition module enables employees to receive acknowledgment for their work and contributions. The grievance module provides a formal route for employees to raise concerns and for administrators to track resolution progress.

### 4.4 Database Design

The database design supports the storage and retrieval of engagement-related data. Tables are created for employees, announcements, surveys, responses, feedback, recognition records, rewards, grievances, and notifications. These tables are linked through relational keys to preserve data integrity and allow efficient retrieval of records.

A well-structured database design is essential to maintain accurate system operations and support reporting and analytics. It also allows the system to produce summaries and dashboard analytics that help administrators understand engagement patterns across the organization.

### 4.5 User Interface Design

The user interface is designed to be intuitive, clean, and easy to navigate. The dashboard presents important engagement metrics, while individual modules provide specific functions such as communication, surveys, and grievance management. The interface is organized to minimize confusion and ensure that employees can access the features they need without difficulty.

The overall design prioritizes usability, allowing both employees and administrators to interact with the system efficiently. A clear interface also strengthens the user experience and encourages more active participation in engagement activities.

## Chapter 5: Results and Discussion

### 5.1 System Implementation Results

The developed HRMS engagement module successfully integrates key employee engagement functions into a unified platform. The system allows employees to access announcements, participate in surveys, provide feedback, and submit grievances. At the same time, administrators can manage communication, monitor engagement activities, and review reported issues. The platform also includes a dashboard that summarizes the number of announcements, surveys, feedback entries, recognitions, grievances, and notifications.

### 5.2 Discussion of Features

The communication module improves information sharing by allowing management to post announcements and provide updates to employees. This helps ensure that employees remain informed about organizational activities, company policies, and important events. The survey and feedback modules provide a structured way to collect employee opinions and evaluate engagement levels. These features allow HR personnel to identify concerns and determine areas that may need improvement.

The recognition module supports a culture of appreciation by allowing employees to receive acknowledgment for performance and contribution. This encourages motivation and strengthens employee loyalty. The grievance module provides employees with a formal channel to raise concerns and allows administrators to monitor and manage issues more effectively. This contributes to a better employee relations environment and helps build trust between employees and management.

### 5.3 Evaluation of System Effectiveness

The system is effective because it centralizes engagement-related tasks that are often handled separately in traditional systems. Instead of using multiple disconnected tools, employees and HR staff can work within a single platform. This improves efficiency, simplifies information flow, and supports real-time participation. It also promotes transparency, which is essential for building trust and improving employee experience.

The results show that the system is capable of supporting employee engagement in a practical and accessible manner. It demonstrates that digital systems can play a meaningful role in human resource management, particularly in delivering communication, recognition, and grievance support.

### 5.4 Limitations and Potential Improvements

Although the current system is functional, there are still opportunities for improvement. Future enhancements may include advanced analytics, automated notification scheduling, more detailed reporting tools, and improved role-based security features. Additional integration with other HR modules could also enhance the overall HRMS and provide a more complete employee experience.

Overall, the implementation demonstrates that the system can contribute positively to employee engagement and strengthen employee relations within the organization.
